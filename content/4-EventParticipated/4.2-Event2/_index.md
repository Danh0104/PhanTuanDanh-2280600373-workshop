### Event Objectives

The presentations focused on sharing practical experience in building AI products on AWS through real projects developed during Agentic AI Build Week. The content covered not only product ideas, but also problem analysis, architecture design, MVP development, cost optimization, product demos, and lessons learned during implementation.

For a Cloud and DevOps-oriented intern, the event helped me understand how AI can be combined with AWS services to solve real business problems. Through the examples presented, I learned that AI is not limited to answering questions. It can also support requirement analysis, system design, data processing, operational monitoring, and decision support.

### Main Event Content

#### Signal Scout

Signal Scout is an AI platform designed to help businesses detect early strategic changes by collecting and analyzing multiple data sources. The system focuses on detecting signals related to organizational restructuring, analyzing operational indicators, building scenarios, and visualizing results on dashboards for strategy and risk management teams.

The team also shared the deployment architecture, AWS cost estimation, and development lessons learned while building the system.

#### Solution Architect Professional Native App

This presentation introduced an AI application that supports the work of a Solution Architect. The system can analyze requirements in natural language, suggest architecture options, generate AWS and Draw.io architecture diagrams, estimate AWS deployment costs, and identify missing requirements in project documentation.

This session helped me understand how AI can support technical tasks that usually take significant time, such as reading documents, designing systems, and preparing customer-facing architecture materials.

#### One Team - AI Conversational Ordering

This presentation introduced the KFC Bot Agent, an AI assistant that helps customers order food directly through messaging platforms such as Zalo, Messenger, and other channels without requiring a separate application.

What impressed me was that the AI did not make decisions blindly. It understood the customer's request, then used tools to check real data such as menu items, promotions, and cart information before confirming the result. The team also shared a design approach that makes the system easier to extend with more channels, features, and services.

#### Hackathon Journey

The final presentation shared the Hackathon journey of the team behind S.H.E.P.H.E.R.D. The system uses Computer Vision and Agentic AI to monitor crowd density, detect congestion, and provide early warnings for operators. The technologies included YOLO, ByteTrack, Amazon SageMaker, Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, Strands Agent, and a React Dashboard.

The team also discussed challenges such as first-time exposure to AI, first-time AWS usage, time pressure, technical issues, and teamwork experience during the Hackathon.

### Key Takeaways

#### Agentic AI

* AI agents can be applied to many areas, including business data analysis, system design support, automated ordering, and operational monitoring.
* AI becomes more effective when combined with appropriate tools and data sources.
* Clear separation of responsibilities between system components makes products easier to extend and maintain.

#### System Design

* An AI system should be designed based on real user needs.
* The architecture should support scalability and future integration.
* Estimating cost during the design phase helps evaluate project feasibility.

#### MVP Development

* Short development timelines require clear goals.
* Work should be divided reasonably among team members.
* Demo preparation should start early.
* The team should focus on core features before expanding the product.

#### Teamwork

From the Hackathon sharing session, I learned that communication, clear role assignment, and coordination strongly affect product quality. In addition to technical knowledge, teamwork and time management are important factors in project success.

### Personal Experience

This event gave me practical knowledge about building AI products on AWS. What impressed me most was that the speakers presented real projects built by their own teams, from initial ideas to implementation challenges.

Through the sessions, I understood that building an AI product is not only about the AI model. It also depends on architecture design, scalability, cost optimization, teamwork, and the full product development process.

Besides technical knowledge, I also learned more about problem-solving mindset, time management, and the importance of learning from community and Hackathon activities.

### Practical Applications

* Learn more about Agentic AI and AI applications on AWS.
* Apply system design thinking to the Netflop project.
